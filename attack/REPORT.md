## Findings

**F1 (exhaustive, independently cross-checked):** From scratch, enumeration of regular subgroups of Hol(G) modulo Aut(G) gives skew-brace counts s(n) and ordinary-brace counts b(n) for *every* additive group at orders 1-15, and separately 20, 21 and 22. The order-20 value b(20)=11 verifies the smallest tested instance (p=5) of Conjecture 6.2. The 2017 paper already verified many more primes, so this is an independent replication, not a new limit. At orders 12 (m=3) and 20 (m=5), q(12)=q(20)=2; at each order exactly one quaternion ordinary brace exists on each of the two abelian additive group types (C_12 and C_6 x C_2; C_20 and C_10 x C_2), relevant to 6.6-6.8/2.28-2.29. This is below the range originally tested in the paper.

**F2 (verified explicit witness, known phenomenon):** The targeted nonabelian additive group C_8 semidirect C_2 with inversion action by 5 (mod 8), order 16, has **66** isomorphism classes of skew braces. **Four** have associative `a*b=-a+a o b-b` but are not two-sided. The JSON `witness16.json` provides *both complete Cayley tables*, without dependency on GAP. A *separate* checker proves both group laws, the left brace identity, associativity of star, and exhibits a violation of the right brace identity at (a,b,c)=(1,1,1) (LHS=3, RHS=7), using zero-based table indices. It also checks the nondegenerate YBE on all 4,096 triples (the canonical brace solution) and non-involutivity. These counterexamples are **not new**: Konovalov--Smoktunowicz--Vendramin, *On skew braces and their ideals*, arXiv:1804.04106, Section 1, reports eight such skew-brace classes at order 16. Our probe reconstructs four from one additive group, and two from another, not the full eight.

These are **seven** chosen additive group presentations, not the complete set of 14 groups of order 16. Total 905 skew braces over these seven types. The second probe's 2 witnesses are not separately stored; the program can reconstruct them.

**F4 (negative searches with precise scope):** Among all ordinary left braces of orders 1-15, 20, 21 and 22, no associative-* non-two-sided example exists; these finite checks are consistent with Lau's theorem. Among all skew braces of orders 1-15, none exists; the 2017/2019 size-16 example is thus minimal *within these computed orders*. All finite groups (including nonabelian ones) of order <=12 and orders 20, 21, 22 occur as the multiplicative group of at least one ordinary left brace in the computed results; this does **not** resolve 2019 Problems 2.31-2.35.

## Evidence

Reproduce with Python >=3.9, standard library only, from inside `attack/`:

```sh
python3 enumerate.py --max-order 12 --seconds 900 --output results-12.json --verbose > run-12.log
python3 enumerate.py --orders 13,14,15,20,21,22 --seconds 600 --output results-more.json --verbose > run-more.log
python3 crosscheck.py > crosscheck.log
python3 probe16.py > probe16.log
python3 probe16_more.py > probe16_more.log
python3 check_witness.py witness16.json > check_witness.log
sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS
```

The enumeration is **not** just a search for examples. For fixed additive G, let `lambda_a` be the holomorph automorphism in the unique element `(a,lambda_a)` of a regular subgroup H. Define `a o b = a + lambda_a(b)`. Closure of H shows the circle group law and `lambda_{a o b}=lambda_a lambda_b`. Since each lambda is additive automorphism, `a o (b+c)=(a o b)-a+(a o c)`. Conversely a skew left brace gives such a regular subgroup by its lambda maps. Two regular subgroups define isomorphic braces **iff** conjugate under Aut(G), because a brace isomorphism must be an additive group automorphism and preserve lambda. The program generates a regular H by adjoining elements to subgroups; every subgroup can be generated by a chain starting at identity, and an element of H outside a proper regular partial subgroup always has a previously unused translation coordinate. The element's order must divide |G|. Its orbit is reduced modulo *all* Aut(G) conjugations at every stage, with no loss of terminal orbits. The finite group catalog for orders used here is complete (the standard classification of groups of orders 1-15, 20-22); **no completeness assertion** is made about the order-16 targeted family.

Independent method: `crosscheck.py` enumerates every assignment of lambda automorphisms and checks the cocycle/group-closure equation directly, rather than extending subgroups; the complete results agree for all additive groups of orders 1-6 and for C8. `check_witness.py` does not import `enumerate.py` and checks 100% of finite triples using literal JSON Cayley tables. The per-group counts are recorded in the logs; the larger intermediate `results-12.json`/`results-more.json` are reproducible output artifacts not committed (avoid unnecessary bulk). All committed source/log/data files other than `SHA256SUMS` are covered by SHA-256 hashes in `SHA256SUMS`. CPU time is printed in logs and far below the 30-minute budget (wall-time measurement, not standardized across machines).

## Not covered

No full enumeration at order 16, nor exhaustive search of quaternion braces beyond m=5. No full classification of cycle sets or all YBE solutions, infinite/free braces, simple braces, group-theoretic unique-product/torsion questions, or knot invariants. No conjecture is claimed proved by finite checking. Publications since 2019 solve some of the listed statements; the list is historically faithful, not a list guaranteed open in 2026.
